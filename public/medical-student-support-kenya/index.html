Medical School Can Be Challenging. You Do Not Have to Figure Everything Out Alone.

Medical training brings lectures, examinations, clinical rotations, ward rounds, logbooks, and the pressure to keep improving. Sometimes you need a study resource. Sometimes you need to compare approaches with another student. And sometimes you simply need a conversation with someone who understands the journey.

I am an MBChB student at Mount Kenya University (MKU) and the person behind OmpathStudy, a growing medical-learning platform. I welcome respectful conversations with medical students across Kenya about learning, study routines, clinical training, and the challenges of medical school.

What can we talk about?

  • Planning revision for MBChB assessments, CATs, and end-of-semester examinations.
  • Making ward rounds, clerking, logbooks, and bedside learning more manageable.
  • Moving from preclinical subjects to clinical medicine and case-based reasoning.
  • Managing academic pressure and building a realistic study routine.
  • Finding medical notes, MCQs, flashcards, and other learning resources.
  • Sharing experiences with peers from MKU and other Kenyan medical schools.

You do not need to attend MKU to reach out. Students from other medical schools are welcome. You can start with one specific question or simply introduce yourself.

Peer support, not a clinical consultation

I can share my experience as a fellow student and point you toward educational resources. This does not replace lecturers, clinical supervisors, qualified clinicians, or mental health professionals. Do not send patient-identifiable information, clinical records, or confidential university material.

Learning support through OmpathStudy

OmpathStudy is being developed to make medical learning more organized and accessible, with study notes, practice questions, explanations, flashcards, and revision tools. It complements—not replaces—teaching, textbooks, supervision, and clinical judgment.
