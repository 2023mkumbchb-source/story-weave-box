## Definition

Asthma is **a heterogeneous disease, usually characterised by chronic airway inflammation**. It is defined by a history of respiratory symptoms — **wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness and cough** — that **vary over time and in intensity**, together with **variable expiratory airflow limitation**.

Three core features:

- **Airway inflammation** (eosinophils, mast cells, Th2 lymphocytes)
- **Bronchial hyper-responsiveness** to triggers
- **Reversible** airflow obstruction

## Causes and Risk Factors

| Trigger | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Allergens** (house dust mite, pollen, cockroach, pets, moulds) | IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity |
| **Aspirin / NSAIDs** | COX blocked → arachidonic acid shunted to lipoxygenase → ↑ leukotrienes → bronchoconstriction and inflammation |
| **Beta-blockers** (e.g. propranolol) | Block β2 receptors on bronchial smooth muscle → bronchospasm |
| **Viral URTIs** (especially rhinovirus) | Epithelial inflammation → ↑ hyper-responsiveness → bronchoconstriction, mucus, narrowing |
| **Cold air and exercise** | Airway cooling and drying → mast-cell mediator release |
| **Smoke, vaping, pollution, occupational agents** | Irritant / sensitiser effect |
| **Emotional stress** | Vagal and inflammatory pathways |

**Host factors:** atopy, family history (atopic triad — asthma, eczema, allergic rhinitis), obesity, prematurity, maternal smoking.

## Clinical Phenotypes

- **Allergic** — childhood onset, atopy, eosinophilic, good ICS response
- **Non-allergic** — neutrophilic, poorer ICS response
- **Adult (late) onset** — often women, may need higher ICS doses
- **Persistent airflow limitation** — long-standing disease with remodelling
- **Asthma with obesity** — prominent symptoms, little eosinophilia
- **Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (Samter's triad)** — asthma + nasal polyps + aspirin sensitivity

## Immunological Mechanism on Allergen Exposure

1. **Sensitisation:** dendritic cells present allergen → **Th2** differentiation.
2. **Th2 cytokines:** **IL-4** → IgE class switching; **IL-5** → eosinophils; **IL-13** → goblet-cell hyperplasia, mucus, hyper-responsiveness.
3. **Re-exposure:** allergen cross-links IgE on mast cells.
   - **Early phase (minutes):** histamine, leukotrienes, PGD2 → bronchospasm
   - **Late phase (4–8 h):** eosinophil influx → sustained inflammation
4. **Remodelling:** sub-epithelial fibrosis, smooth-muscle hypertrophy, mucous-gland hyperplasia.

## Pathophysiology

**1. Bronchial wall oedema and inflammation** → airway narrowing → dyspnoea, **hyper-resonance** (air trapping), **wheeze** (difficulty getting air out).

**2. Mucus hypersecretion** — activated goblet cells secrete mucus that narrows the airway further; it irritates receptors and triggers cough.

**3. Bronchoconstriction and bronchospasm** — O₂ in and CO₂ out are impaired; **expiration is compromised more** → air trapping → alveoli balloon out → **hyperinflated lungs**.

## Complications

### 1. Respiratory failure
Especially in **status asthmaticus**. Air trapping → hypoventilation → **hypoxia and hypercapnia** (respiratory acidosis). The patient compensates with ↑ respiratory rate and accessory-muscle use.

> **Exam pearl:** a **normal or rising PaCO₂** in an acute attack is ominous — the patient is tiring.

### 2. Pneumothorax
Hyperinflation raises the risk of apical rupture → absent breath sounds on one side, tracheal deviation, sudden hypoxaemia.

### 3. Pulsus paradoxus
Fall of **>10 mmHg** in systolic BP on inspiration. Exaggerated negative intrathoracic pressure ↑ right-heart venous return → septum shifts into the LV → ↓ LV filling and stroke volume.

### Others
Pneumomediastinum, subcutaneous emphysema, mucus plugging with lobar collapse, ABPA, steroid side-effects, death.

## Clinical Presentation

### History
- Wheeze, cough, chest tightness, dyspnoea, exhaustion
- **More likely asthma if symptoms:** worse at night/early morning; vary over time and intensity; triggered by viral infection, exercise, allergens, weather change, laughter or irritants
- Family history — atopic triad
- Drug history — aspirin, NSAIDs, beta-blockers

### Examination
- Signs of respiratory distress
- ↑ **anteroposterior diameter** (hyperinflation)
- Hyper-resonant percussion
- Polyphonic **expiratory wheeze**
- **Silent chest** — so little air entry no wheeze is heard (life-threatening)
- Nose — allergic rhinitis, nasal polyps

## Diagnostic Approach

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Chest X-ray** | Normal or hyperinflation; excludes pneumothorax and pneumonia |
| **ECG** | Usually normal; sinus tachycardia in attacks |
| **ABG** | Low pH, ↑ pCO₂ in severe attacks |
| **Spirometry** | ↓ FEV₁, mildly ↓ FVC → **FEV₁/FVC < 0.70** |
| **Reversibility** | FEV₁ ↑ **>12% and >200 mL** after salbutamol |
| **Methacholine challenge** | FEV₁ falls **≥20%** |
| **Peak expiratory flow** | Diurnal variability >10%; serial readings |
| **FeNO** | Raised (>40–50 ppb) supports eosinophilic inflammation |
| **Eosinophils, IgE, skin-prick tests** | Phenotyping |

## Grading an Acute Attack

| Severity | Features |
|---|---|
| **Moderate** | PEF 50–75% best/predicted |
| **Acute severe** | PEF 33–50%, RR ≥25, HR ≥110, cannot complete sentences |
| **Life-threatening** | PEF <33%, SpO₂ <92%, **normal PaCO₂**, silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion, arrhythmia, hypotension, confusion |
| **Near-fatal** | **Raised PaCO₂** or need for ventilation |

### Emergency management (mnemonic **O SHIT ME**)
1. **O**xygen — SpO₂ 94–98%
2. **S**albutamol 5 mg nebulised (back-to-back)
3. **H**ydrocortisone 100 mg IV or prednisolone 40–50 mg PO for 5 days
4. **I**pratropium 0.5 mg nebulised
5. **T**heophylline/aminophylline — senior decision
6. **M**agnesium sulphate 1.2–2 g IV over 20 min
7. **E**scalate — ICU, intubation

Exclude pneumothorax; never sedate. Discharge when PEF >75%, inhaler technique checked and a written action plan given.

## Paediatric Asthma

- Under 5: clinical diagnosis (recurrent wheeze, response to treatment); spirometry unreliable
- Spacer with face mask
- Distinguish from viral-induced wheeze, bronchiolitis, foreign body

## Management

### Non-pharmacological
- Avoid occupational and domestic allergens/irritants — cockroaches, rodents, dust mites, air pollution
- Avoid drugs that worsen asthma
- Manage emotional stress
- **Stop smoking**, second-hand smoke and vaping
- Vaccination, weight loss, written action plan

### Pharmacological
Always **check inhaler technique and adherence** before stepping up.

| Step | Treatment (lecture scheme) |
|---|---|
| **1** | SABA as needed — salbutamol (β2-agonist) |
| **2** | Add **low-dose ICS** |
| **3** | Low-dose ICS + **LABA** (or medium-dose ICS) |
| **4** | LABA + **medium-dose ICS** |
| **5** | LABA + **high-dose ICS** (± LAMA, refer) |
| **6** | Add **oral corticosteroids** |

**GINA preferred track:** as-needed **low-dose ICS–formoterol** at Steps 1–2, then maintenance-and-reliever therapy (MART) at Steps 3–5. SABA-only treatment is no longer recommended for adults and adolescents.

**Add-ons:** **montelukast** (especially aspirin-induced and allergic asthma), tiotropium, biologics — **omalizumab** (anti-IgE), **mepolizumab/benralizumab** (anti-IL-5), **dupilumab** (anti-IL-4Rα). Step down after 3 months of good control.

## Asthma vs COPD

| Feature | Asthma | COPD |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Often childhood | >40 years, smoker |
| Symptoms | Variable, nocturnal | Progressive, persistent |
| Reversibility | Marked | Limited |
| Inflammation | Eosinophilic, Th2 | Neutrophilic, CD8+ |
| Mainstay | ICS | Bronchodilators |

## High-Yield Review

- Asthma = **variable symptoms + variable expiratory airflow**.
- Aspirin → leukotriene excess; beta-blockers → β2 blockade.
- **Silent chest, normal/rising PaCO₂, PEF <33%** = life-threatening.
- Pulsus paradoxus >10 mmHg = severe asthma.
- Reversibility: FEV₁ ↑ >12% and >200 mL.
