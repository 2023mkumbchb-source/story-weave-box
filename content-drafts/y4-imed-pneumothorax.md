## Introduction

- **Laennec** described the clinical picture of pneumothorax in **1819**.
- **Kjaergard (1932)** gave the modern description of primary spontaneous pneumothorax in healthy people.
- Incidence of primary pneumothorax: **18–28 per 100,000/year in men** and **1.2–6 per 100,000/year in women**.

## Definition

Pneumothorax is the **presence of air or gas in the pleural space** — air outside the lung, within the pleural cavity.

## Mechanism

![Air entering the pleural space — collapsed lung](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s5-1-0f5046b7.jpg)

- Normal pleural pressure is **negative throughout the respiratory cycle** (about −5 cmH₂O).
- It results from two opposing forces: the **outward pull of the chest wall** and the **elastic recoil of the lung**.
- When a communication forms between an alveolus (or the atmosphere) and the pleural space, **air flows in until the pressure difference disappears or the leak seals**.

## Pathophysiology

**Loss of negative pressure** → lung recoils → ↓ lung volume → **low V/Q, anatomical shunt → hypoxia**; ↓ vital capacity, ↓ PaO₂.

**Positive pressure (tension)** → compresses great veins and heart → impaired venous return → ↓ cardiac output → **hypotension** (obstructive shock).

## Classification

![Classification of pneumothorax](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s7-1-97689521.jpg)

```text
Pneumothorax
├── Spontaneous
│   ├── Primary (no lung disease)
│   └── Secondary (underlying lung disease)
└── Acquired
    ├── Traumatic: blunt / penetrating
    └── Iatrogenic: procedures, positive pressure ventilation
```

### Primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP)
Young healthy people; **rupture of apical subpleural blebs or bullae**.
- **Smoking** (up to 20-fold risk)
- **Tall, thin males**
- Distal airway inflammation
- Structural bronchial abnormalities
- Genetic contribution (e.g. Birt–Hogg–Dubé)

### Secondary spontaneous pneumothorax (SSP)

| Common causes | Rarer causes |
|---|---|
| **COPD** (most common) | Interstitial lung disease, sarcoidosis |
| **Tuberculosis** | Eosinophilic granuloma / histiocytosis X |
| **Asthma** | Lymphangioleiomyomatosis |
| Suppurative pneumonia | AIDS (*Pneumocystis* pneumonia) |
| Cystic fibrosis | Lung carcinoma, chemotherapy |
| | Scleroderma, **Marfan syndrome**, rheumatoid disease |
| | Pulmonary infarct, granulomatosis with polyangiitis |

> SSP is more dangerous than PSP — poor lung reserve means even a small one may cause respiratory failure.

### Traumatic pneumothorax
- **Accidental:** blunt trauma with rib fractures; stab or gunshot wounds
- **Iatrogenic:** **positive pressure ventilation** (alveolar rupture → interstitial emphysema → often bilateral); biopsy, thoracocentesis, CVP line, tracheostomy

## Clinical Features

**Constant:** sudden **dyspnoea** and **pleuritic chest pain**.
**Uncommon:** cough, haemoptysis, orthopnoea, cyanosis, tachycardia, anxiety, vague malaise.

### Physical examination
Depends on size; vital signs often normal in small ones.
- ↓ chest movement on the affected side
- Trachea shifted **away** if large
- **Tactile fremitus absent**
- **Hyper-resonant** percussion
- Breath sounds **reduced or absent**
- Liver edge displaced down with a right-sided pneumothorax
- Subcutaneous emphysema (crackling)

### Tension pneumothorax
Classically **hypotension + hypoxia**; life-threatening.
- Rapidly progressive breathlessness, circulatory collapse (tachycardia, hypotension, sweating)
- **Distended neck veins**
- Tracheal deviation (late)
- X-ray: mediastinal shift, flattened hemidiaphragm, lung collapse
- More common with **positive pressure ventilation** and **trauma**

**In ICU:** suspect in any ventilated or CPR patient who suddenly deteriorates — dyspnoea, cyanosis, marked tachycardia, hypotension, **airway pressure alarms triggered**.

> **Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis — decompress before any X-ray.**

### Chest X-ray (erect PA)

![Chest X-ray in erect and supine positions](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s21-1-a9eeced2.jpg)
- **Visceral pleural line** with **no lung markings** beyond it
- BTS size cut-off: **2 cm rim** at the hilum
- **Supine film:** **deep sulcus sign**, hyperlucent hemithorax

### CT chest

![Pneumothorax on CT](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s26-1-3f6ac974.jpg)
- Lungs obscured by surgical emphysema
- Distinguishes pneumothorax from a large **bulla**
- Occult pneumothorax, surgical planning

### Bedside ultrasound
Loss of **lung sliding**, absent B-lines, barcode sign; **lung point** is diagnostic.

## Management

**Goals:** re-expand the lung, remove the cause, prevent recurrence.

### BTS approach

![BTS guideline for spontaneous pneumothorax](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s28-1-f3f2dfcb.jpg)

```text
Spontaneous pneumothorax
  ├── Bilateral or unstable → CHEST DRAIN
  ├── PRIMARY
  │     ├── Not breathless, rim <2 cm → discharge, review 2–4 weeks
  │     └── Breathless or rim >2 cm → ASPIRATE (16–18G)
  │            ├── Success → discharge, review
  │            └── Failure → chest drain
  └── SECONDARY
        ├── Rim >2 cm or breathless → CHEST DRAIN
        ├── Rim 1–2 cm → aspirate; if fails, drain; admit 24 h
        └── Rim <1 cm → admit, high-flow O2, observe
```

### Oxygen therapy
High-concentration O₂ **lowers pleural capillary nitrogen**, increasing the gradient for absorption. Natural resorption is **1.25–1.8% of hemithorax volume per 24 h**; oxygen gives about a **4-fold increase**. Caution in CO₂-retaining COPD.

### Simple aspiration
- **First-line for all PSP needing intervention**
- In SSP only for **small (<2 cm), minimally breathless patients under 50**; admit and observe **≥24 h**
- Repeat aspiration reasonable in PSP if <2.5 L removed the first time

### Needle decompression (tension)
14–16G cannula, **2nd intercostal space mid-clavicular line** or **4th–5th space just anterior to mid-axillary line**; always follow with a chest drain.

### Intercostal tube drainage

![The safe triangle for chest drain insertion](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s34-1-c26fd5c1.jpg)

![Intercostal drain with underwater seal](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-ptx-s33-1-60f3f0d8.jpg)

**Indications:** unstable pneumothorax, severe dyspnoea, large collapse, open or tension pneumothorax, recurrence, failed aspiration, bilateral, ventilated patients, haemopneumothorax.

**Safe triangle**
- Anterior: lateral border of **pectoralis major**
- Posterior: lateral border of **latissimus dorsi** (mid-axillary line)
- Inferior: **nipple level (5th intercostal space)**
- Apex: below the axilla
- Insert **above the upper border of the rib**

**Underwater seal:** bottle primed with ~200 mL saline; fluid level should **swing with respiration**.

**No bubbling may mean:** the lung re-expanded, the tube is blocked, or the tube holes lie in the chest wall. Remove ~**24 h after re-expansion**. Never clamp a bubbling drain.

### Surgical treatment

**Indications:** no response to medical treatment, **persistent air leak (>3–5 days)**, haemopneumothorax, **bilateral** or **recurrent** pneumothorax, tension that fails to drain, thickened pleura preventing re-expansion, multiple blebs, high-risk jobs (pilots, divers).

**Pleurectomy and pleurodesis** by **VATS** or thoracotomy aim to:
1. Seal lung leaks
2. Remove blebs and bullae (**bullectomy**)
3. Fuse visceral to parietal pleura so the lung cannot fully collapse

**Methods:** **pleurectomy** (strip parietal pleura), **pleural abrasion** (scourer), **chemical pleurodesis** (usually **talc**).

## Complications

- Pyopneumothorax, hydropneumothorax, **haemopneumothorax**
- **Mediastinal and subcutaneous emphysema**
- Re-expansion pulmonary oedema
- Bronchopleural fistula, respiratory failure, cardiac arrest

## Recurrence

- After a first episode, about **one-third** recur
- After a second, about **one-half** have a third
- After three, recurrence is likely to continue

**Advice:** stop smoking; no flying until X-ray resolution; diving contraindicated unless surgical pleurectomy.

## High-Yield Review

- PSP: tall, thin young male smoker; apical blebs. SSP: COPD most common.
- Signs: ↓ expansion, **hyper-resonance**, absent fremitus and breath sounds.
- Tension: **hypotension + hypoxia + distended neck veins** — decompress first.
- Size cut-off **2 cm**; PSP → **aspirate first**; SSP >2 cm → **drain**.
- Safe triangle: pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi, 5th intercostal space.
