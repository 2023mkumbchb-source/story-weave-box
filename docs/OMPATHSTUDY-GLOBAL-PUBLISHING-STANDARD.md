# OmpathStudy Global Publishing Standard

This standard applies to all new and updated educational articles across OmpathStudy. Content is universal by default: curriculum year, semester, course code, unit, school and institution are organisational metadata, not restrictions on who may learn from an article.

## Titles and opening
- Use a clear, topic-first, searchable title.
- Do not prefix ordinary learning articles with year, semester, course code, institution or unit.
- Retain exam identifiers only for genuine past papers, CATs and institution-specific assessment resources.
- Begin with a concise subject introduction or definition, not administrative scope metadata.

## Structure and readability
- Use descriptive heading hierarchy, short paragraphs and visible spacing.
- Use bullets for lists, numbered steps for sequences, and tables for meaningful comparisons.
- Avoid redundant headings, dense paragraphs, excessive nested bullets and decorative separators.
- Present learning objectives as separate, assessable checklist items under a clearly labelled heading.
- Preserve adequate clinical depth; do not flatten a topic into shallow bullet points.

## Images and visual evidence
Minimum relevant image count is based on article length:
- Up to 800 words: at least 1 image.
- 801–1,800 words: at least 2 images.
- 1,801–3,000 words: at least 3 images.
- 3,001–4,500 words: at least 4 images.
- More than 4,500 words: at least 5 images.

Place visuals beside the section they explain. Prefer clinically meaningful diagrams, anatomy/pathology images, procedural illustrations and useful charts. Avoid generic, ambiguous, duplicated or decorative photos. Use properly sourced/licensed visuals; never invent image URLs or citations. An editor must review relevance, not merely image count.

## SEO and curriculum mapping
- Keep topic keywords in the title, headings and SEO description naturally.
- Keep year/unit/course mapping in database metadata, tags or curriculum relationships where useful.
- Do not let curriculum labels make universal content appear restricted to a single student cohort.

## Publication gate
Before an educational article is published, check title/opening, headings and spacing, checklist-style learning outcomes, clinical completeness, image count and relevance, source quality, and SEO metadata. Drafts may be saved while work is incomplete; published status should only be enabled after the checklist passes.
